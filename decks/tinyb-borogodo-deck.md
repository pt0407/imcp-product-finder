## SLIDE 4: I. Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Business problem** | Mass chocolate is losing units. Chocolate candy units fell 12.4% from 2021 to 2025 while dollar sales rose on price alone. Brigadeiro, Brazil's birthday candy, is largely unknown to U.S. consumers (tinyB founder: "No one here knew brigadeiro"). |
| **Business need** | tinyB has a proven product and a loyal base (40+ employees, $2.4M+ trailing revenue as of 2022) but is known mainly in the Bay Area and through corporate workshops. It needs a consumer-facing story that drives trial beyond its current channels. |
| **Solution** | **Borogodó**: position tinyB as the premium, story-led chocolate that justifies its price with the story behind it, and turn the brand's own corporate workshop format into a consumer sampling and UGC engine. |
| **Objective (45 days)** | 6,000 orders, 3M impressions, 1,500 UGC submissions |

*Theme tag used on every activity slide: **Borogodó***

## SLIDE 5: II. Product Description
**Hero SKU: tinyB brigadeiros (Bossa Collection starter box)**
- Brazil's classic chocolate candy, reformulated for U.S. palates: "less sugar, better chocolate" (tinyB, About Us)
- Handmade in small batches in San Mateo, CA
- Gluten-free; "Brazilian-inspired flavors"; giftable box format
- Other lines: chocolate spreads (*brigadeiro de colher*), cookies, DIY kits, team-building events

**Why it wins on product:** a new category for most U.S. consumers, a clean-label story, and built-in gifting appeal.

## SLIDE 8: IV. Target Market — Primary
**"Sweet Explorers": 22–38, urban and suburban (Bay Area, NYC, LA, Austin), food-curious, gift-buyers**

| Dimension | Profile |
|---|---|
| Demographics | Gen Z and Millennial; household income $75K+ **[VERIFY segment size]** |
| Behavior | Discover food on TikTok and Instagram; buy premium and experiential treats |
| Psychographics | Want treats that feel personal; value story and craft over volume |
| Media | TikTok (primary), Instagram Reels, email |

**Supporting data:**
- 51% of Gen Z bought a food or beverage product in the past year after seeing it on social media, vs. 27% of other age groups (Tinuiti, 2023)
- 55% of Gen Z use social media to discover new foods and recipes; 69% of Gen Z use TikTok for this (Hartman Group)
- 63% of Gen Z have tried a new flavor or cuisine because of something they saw online (Publicis Commerce / OnePoll, Aug 2025)

## SLIDE 9: IV. Target Market — Secondary and Market Size
**Secondary 1: "Gift Givers":** adults buying for birthdays, hostess gifts, and holidays
**Secondary 2: "Brazilian and Latin American Diaspora":** cultural anchor and word-of-mouth. Brigadeiro is a birthday staple in Brazil (Estadão).

**Market context:**
- U.S. confectionery sales hit a record $55B in 2025; 99.8% of households bought confectionery (NCA)
- Chocolate is 51.7% of confectionery sales (NCA / Euromonitor)
- **Super-premium chocolate grew 16.7% in dollar sales in 2025; premium grew 9.8%; mainstream grew 3.8%** (NielsenIQ)

*Implication: premium is where growth is. The campaign targets the premium tier, not the candy aisle.*

## SLIDE 10: V. Research and Insights (1 of 2): Discovery
**Method:** Desk research of published industry reports (see bibliography). Week 0 plan: 30 intercept interviews at a sampling event to validate the primary segment.

| Insight | Source | → Therefore we will |
|---|---|---|
| Social drives Gen Z food purchase: 51% vs. 27% | Tinuiti 2023 | Lead with creator content, not TV or print |
| TikTok is a leading discovery platform for Gen Z food: 69% of Gen Z use it for food discovery | NCA 2026 ("a top destination for discovery"); Hartman Group | Build the campaign around TikTok creator seeding and a UGC challenge |
| Gen Z and Millennials over-index on novel, experiential candy | NCA 2026 | Position brigadeiro as a new experience, not a candy bar |

## SLIDE 11: V. Research and Insights (2 of 2): Premium and Sampling
| Insight | Source | → Therefore we will |
|---|---|---|
| Super-premium and premium are the fastest-growing chocolate tiers in dollars (+16.7%, +9.8%) | NIQ, Feb 2026 | Price on story and craft, not discounts |
| Chocolate units fell 12.4% (2021–2025); category is price-driven | Euromonitor / Confectionery News, May 2026 | Sell "fewer, higher-stakes purchases": gifting and experiences, not everyday volume |
| Sampling drives trial: 58% cumulative trial over 20 weeks for sampled items (vendor-commissioned study) | PromoWorks R.I.S.E. | Put tinyB in people's hands through live demos |
| Demo conversion: 22.25% sales-to-sampling in a SoCal Whole Foods case (vendor case study) | Attack! Marketing | Plan on 15% sample-to-purchase, a conservative target below the vendor benchmarks |
| Free sampling is "very effective in inducing trial" in controlled U.S. grocery field study | Journal study, DOI 10.1108/00070701111177674 | Use in-store demos as the core trial mechanic |

**Caveat on vendor data:** PromoWorks and Attack! are commercial vendors, so their benchmarks are optimistic. Our targets are set below them.

## SLIDE 12: VI. Campaign Strategy
**Theme: Borogodó**
Brazilian Portuguese for the little extra that makes something special: a surprise, a warmth, a joy you didn't expect (per tinyB's own copy).

**Positioning:** "Chocolate with a story you can taste."

**Three pillars (every activity maps to one):**
1. **Taste it** — Borogodó Hour live demos (trial)
2. **Share it** — #MyMomsBorogodó family-sweets UGC challenge (engagement, originality)
3. **Gift it** — Borogodó gift boxes for Valentine's Day, the peak chocolate gifting moment (NCA: big four seasons = 63% of sales)

## SLIDE 14: VI. Activations (Named Programs)
**A. Borogodó Hour (in-store and corporate demos)**
- 12 live demos in Bay Area grocers (SF, Oakland, San Jose, Palo Alto) **[VERIFY venue partners]**
- Guests roll their own brigadeiro with Renata's recipe card; "make one, take one"
- Format adapted from tinyB's existing corporate workshops (verified on tinyB's site and G1)

**B. #MyMomsBorogodó (UGC challenge)**
- Post a sweet your mom or grandmother made, and tag it with a tinyB brigadeiro
- Top 10 get a Bossa box; one winner gets a team Borogodó workshop for their office
- Judging: tinyB founders + a guest food creator

**C. Creator seeding**
- 25 micro-creators (5K–50K followers): food, Brazilian-American, and Bay Area lifestyle
- Each receives a Bossa box and a story brief, not a script

**D. Borogodó Letters (email)**
- 3 emails: founder story, the recipe, the challenge winner

| Line item | Amount | % | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|

| Borogodó Hour demos (12 events) | $15,000 | 15% | Trial mechanic; vendor benchmarks support sampling |
| Content production | $12,000 | 12% | TikTok shoot, carousel design, POS |
| Challenge prizes and shipping | $5,000 | 5% | 10 Bossa boxes + winner workshop |
| PR and founder content | $5,000 | 5% | Founder story is the core differentiator |
| Email / CRM tools | $3,000 | 3% | Borogodó Letters |
| Contingency | $5,000 | 5% | Standard reserve |
| **Total** | **$100,000** | **100%** | |

**Financial viability (assumptions [VERIFY with tinyB sales data]):**
- AOV: $45 · Gross margin: 45%
- Gross profit per order: $20.25
- Break-even: $100,000 ÷ $20.25 ≈ **4,940 orders**
- Target: **6,000 orders** → $270K revenue, $121.5K gross profit, **+$21.5K net** after the campaign spend
- Blended CAC: $100,000 ÷ 6,000 = **$16.67 per order**
- Repeat purchases after day 45 are upside and are not counted in this plan.
