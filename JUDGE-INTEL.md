# DECA Judge Intelligence — D5 Texas → States → ICDC

*Compiled Nov 7, 2026. Caveat: DECA does not publish judge demographic breakdowns. This combines official eligibility rules, recruitment materials, judge testimonials, and DECA member stats (proxy for the talent pipeline).*

---

## 1. Who Is Allowed to Judge (Texas DECA official requirements)

From texasdeca.org/judge FAQ:

- **Must be 21+ years old**
- **Industry experience required in:** marketing, management, hospitality, finance, entrepreneurship, sales, human resources, travel & tourism, retail, or accounting
- **No prior judging experience needed** — DECA trains judges on-site with the rubric and evaluation form
- Volunteers, unpaid; in-person or virtual options

**Translation:** Your district judge is likely a working professional or retiree with a business background — not necessarily a marketing specialist. Many are first-time judges.

## 2. Judge Profiles by Competition Level

### District (D5 — Austin/Central TX, Round Rock, Dec 7–8, 2026)

- Recruited locally: Austin-area business professionals, educators, DECA alumni, corporate volunteers, parents
- **Mix of experience levels:** First-timers alongside veteran judges. Training happens same-day before judging.
- **Likely Austin skew:** Austin's economy is tech-heavy — expect judges with tech, startup, and marketing backgrounds. Younger on average than a typical district.
- **Scoring behavior:** Follow the rubric literally. Reward completeness, clarity, confidence. Less likely to reward risky/novel ideas they don't understand.
- **Quote from a D5 judge (LinkedIn):** "I made sure that every student had an equal chance to succeed... greeted every student with a warm smile to help ease their nervousness." → District judges are *encouraging*, not cutthroat.

### State (Texas SCDC — ~5,000+ competitors)

- More experienced judges; many are repeat volunteers who judged district or other states
- Industry professionals recruited statewide; corporate partner judges (e.g., Adobe sponsors IMC events)
- **Scoring behavior:** Calibrated across hundreds of teams. They compare campaigns against each other, not just the rubric. Clear strategy + research connection separates finalists.
- **"Slaughterfest" culture** (per a Texas DECA officer): "the standards for states go up a lot if you're trying to qualify internationally."

### ICDC (International — Atlanta)

- Senior judges: decades of judging experience, DECA officials, industry leaders
- Some are career volunteer judges (one documented judge has served 49 years, 28 ICDCs)
- **Scoring behavior:** Exemplary-or-nothing. They reward genuine innovation, realism ("would a real client buy this?"), and polish. They've seen thousands of campaigns — generic ideas die here.
- Corporate partners may supply subject-matter judges (Adobe sponsors all IMC events)

## 4. What the Data Means for Your Campaign

| Level | Judge psychology | What wins |
|---|---|---|
| **District** | Generalist, encouraging, rubric-literal | **Clarity + completeness.** A product a non-specialist instantly understands. Perfect slide order, no penalties, confident delivery. Don't get clever — get clear. |
| **State** | Experienced, comparative, industry-aware | **Strategy quality.** Research that drives decisions, differentiated idea, professional polish. They compare you to 50+ other IMCP teams. |
| **ICDC** | Veteran, calibrated, "real client" test | **Genuine innovation.** Would a real agency pitch this? Is the idea ownable? They've seen everything — only campaigns with a real point of view place. |

## 5. Strategic Implication for Product Choice

**For a Texas D5 district-first path:**

- **People's Choice Beef Jerky** remains the safest strong pick — instantly understandable to a generalist judge (beef, jerky, family recipe = zero explanation), emotionally resonant (96-year story), and Texas-friendly (beef culture).
- **Snack Hawaii** is a close second — "Hawaiian snacks subscription" is instantly gettable and aspirational.
- **tinyB chocolate** is the highest-risk/highest-reward — moving story but "brigadeiro" requires explanation a district judge may not have patience for.
- **Boba Tea Protein** — Gen Z judges would love it; generalists might find "protein bubble tea" confusing.

**The district → state → ICDC funnel means:** pick a product that's *easy to explain* (district-friendly) but has *depth to reward* (state/ICDC-friendly). People's Choice and Snack Hawaii both fit; tinyB is riskier at district but stronger at ICDC if you survive.
