## Why Candycopia wins

1. **Best problem→solution fit:** The brand already HAS the magic (custom candy bar experience) but it's trapped in two suburbs. The campaign doesn't invent a gimmick — it scales an existing advantage. Judges love "amplify what's already working."
2. **Most ownable idea:** "Build Your Own Candy Bar" UGC challenge is specific to THIS product — can't be copied by a competitor. The Community Mix subscription creates a recurring revenue story.
3. **Easiest creative samples:** UGC unboxing videos, mix builder screenshots, in-store POS, winner announcements — all highly visual and mockable.
4. **Strong business case:** 297K monthly visits on a tiny team proves pent-up demand; subscription model gives judges a financial-viability story.
5. **Demo is perfect:** 18–34 food-curious Gen Z/millennials who love customization, challenges, and nostalgic candy — exactly who buys this.

## Why the others fell short

- **tinyB:** Beautiful story but product depth/awareness too thin — judges may question if it's "big enough" for a campaign. Great backup if Candycopia falls through.
- **Snack Hawaii:** Strong whitespace but niche/regional product limits mass appeal; "Hawaii" angle is tourism-dependent.
- **People's Choice:** Heritage story is good but jerky is a crowded category; differentiation harder.
- **Boba Tea Protein:** Product-market fit unproven; weakest brand/company data in the set.

## LOCKED IN: Candycopia

**Campaign:** "Build Your Own Candy Bar"
**Core mechanic:** UGC challenge → customers design mixes → best mixes become limited-run "Community Mixes" → subscription product
**45-day timeline:** Launch challenge + mix builder → creator seeding → UGC push → first Community Mix drop → subscription push → PR
**Budget:** $100K (30% paid social, 20% creators, 15% web tool, 15% content, 10% PR, 5% contingency, 5% events)
**KPIs:** 1M UGC engagements, 10M impressions, 15K mix orders, 1K subscribers
