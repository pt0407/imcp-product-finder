# Plan: The Best IMCP Presentation (2026–27 season)

**Built only from:** the 1st/2nd/3rd-place ICDC 2026 decks, the 3rd-place score sheet, the 2021 Bayview written entry, and DECA's official 2026–27 IMC guidelines, evaluation form, and penalty checklist.
**Today:** Sat Nov 7, 2026 · **Prepared deck due: Tue Nov 10** · **District presentation (TX D5):** Dec 7–8, 2026, Round Rock

---

## 1. What the data says

1. **The rubric changed this year.** The winning decks were scored on last year's 13 criteria. The 2026–27 form (DECA Guide pp. 105–108) is different:

   | Category | Points |
   |---|---|
   | Knowledge of industry/brand | 5 |
   | Business opportunity | 5 |
   | **Research, insights, connection to campaign** | **15** |
   | **Campaign strategy** | **15** |
   | **Campaign activities and schedule** | **15** |
   | Proposed budget | 10 |
   | Impact and measurement | 10 |
   | Organization / Effectiveness / Delivery / Design / Overall impression | 5 each (25) |
   | **Total** | **100** |

   "Unifying theme" and "creativity" are no longer their own line items. Creativity now sits inside Campaign strategy ("using creativity and innovative ideas"). Research is new and tied for the largest bucket.

2. **Last year's winners had almost no research.** None of the three decks has a research section. The closest is one "Why B2C now?" box (1st), a SWOT (3rd), and problem facts (2nd). None explains a research method. The 2026–27 form scores exactly that: "Explains the research method, results, and how the findings inform the next steps." **This is the biggest open opportunity: 15 points that last year's winners would not have earned.**

3. **The top two decks had a specific, structural business problem. The 3rd had a generic one.**
   - 1st, StixFresh: "only sold B2B despite consumer interest" → expand into B2C.
   - 2nd, MarkSetBot: needs a larger share of revenue from direct bot sales.
   - 3rd, Misfits: "lack of brand positioning/awareness." Its overview scored in the **30th percentile** at prelims, its weakest criterion.

4. **The top two decks also had a measurable mission layer.** "100,000 lbs of fruit saved" (1st) and "prevent 1.2 tons of CO2" (2nd). Both are sustainability missions that judges can picture.

5. **Execution standard (all three decks):**
   - Exactly 20 slides.
   - 62–92 words per slide.
   - Real-looking mockups on most slides: phone screens with SMS threads, a co-branded HelloFresh box, designed stickers, a branded PR box.
   - Named programs ("Earth Day Trial Fleet", "#BeAMisfit", "Secret Sticker Squad").
   - KPI targets per objective.

6. **All three winners competed solo.** A team works, but every member must present and answer questions.

7. **The exam is about a third of the score.** The presentation counts 2x the exam. The 3rd-place team's exam was in the 92.8th percentile.

8. **No food or drinks in the judging room** (official rules). If the product is food, the judge can't taste it. The 3rd-place deck (protein bars) still placed, so this doesn't disqualify food. It just removes one interactive option.

## 2. The strategy in one line

**Match last year's winners on execution, then beat them on research.** That means real primary research, a stated method, and every finding linked to a campaign decision. No winner did this last year, and it is now worth 15 points.

---

## 3. Product selection criteria (derived from the winners)

Score each candidate 0–2 on each criterion. Pick the highest total; break ties on criteria 1 and 4.

| # | Criterion | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Specific structural business problem** (channel gap, revenue mix, new market). Not "awareness." | 1st and 2nd had one. The 3rd's generic problem was its weakest criterion. |
| 2 | **Measurable mission or impact** a judge can picture | 1st: lbs of fruit saved. 2nd: tons of CO2. |
| 3 | **Little-known product with an "aha"** that teaches the judge something | Earns industry-knowledge points. All three products were niche. |
| 4 | **Company access:** you can email or interview someone and get real data (AOV, channels, baseline sales) | Research method plus a credible budget. DECA's own guidance asks "Can I get information from the company?" |
| 5 | **Visual product** for mockups | All three decks relied on product mockups. |
| 6 | **Fits IMCP:** retail or e-commerce product | Official definition. |
| 7 | **Not already heavily marketed** | The campaign must add something new. |

**With a Nov 10 deadline, there's no time to wait for company replies.** Pick a product that already has strong public data, or one where a team member personally knows the owner and can get answers this weekend.

## 4. Research plan (the 15-point differentiator)

| Method | Size | Output | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target-audience survey (Google Forms; school group chats, family, social) | n ≥ 50 by Sun night (100 if possible) | Awareness, purchase drivers, channels, price sensitivity | Target market, strategy, channels |
| Prospect interviews (people in the target segment you can reach this weekend) | 3 | Quotes and pain points | Insights, creative copy |
| Company interview | Only if someone on the team knows the owner | Baseline sales, AOV, channels | Budget, KPIs. Otherwise use published data and label assumptions |
| Competitor audit | 3–5 brands | Positioning map, price and channel comparison | Industry knowledge, differentiation |
| Social listening / media-habits review | 1 platform sweep | Where the audience spends time | Channel mix and schedule |
| Secondary data | 5+ sources, each with publisher and date | Market size, trends, cultural/economic/tech factors | Required "influences" analysis |

**Rule:** every insight slide follows "Finding (method, n=) → therefore we will [specific action]." Put the survey instrument and full results on a handout, which judges are allowed to keep.

## 6. Building each category to Exemplary

| Category | Exemplary means | How we get there |
|---|---|---|
| Industry/brand (5) | Thorough understanding of the company, product, and its position | Company interview + competitor map on slide 6 |
| Business opportunity (5) | Opportunity, goals, and target are all clear | Structural problem; four-box overview; SMART objectives |
| Research (15) | Method, results, and how they inform next steps | Primary survey + interviews; "finding → therefore" format |
| Strategy (15) | Feasible, data-based, creative, solves the problem | One big idea that solves the structural problem; one element a competitor couldn't copy |
| Activities (15) | Actionable, realistic timeline, target/channel/activity aligned | Gantt; every program tied to a segment and channel from the research |
| Budget (10) | Justified, key expenses named, viability addressed | Itemized lines with real quotes or benchmarks (as the 2nd place did); funnel math; conservative case |
| Impact (10) | Relevant metrics that capture results | KPI table with baselines from the company and targets tied to the funnel |
| Organization (5) | Logical, easy to follow | Official outline order; consistent section nav bar (the 1st place used one) |
| Effectiveness (5) | Persuades with a clear message | One-sentence campaign thesis repeated at the open and close |
| Delivery (5) | Interactive techniques, e.g., visual storytelling | Hand the judge a printed creative sample; walk through a phone mockup; open with a customer quote from the research |
| Design (5) | Professional visuals that add value | Mockups on ≥10 slides; ~60–90 words per slide; one visual system |
| Overall (5) | Poise, professionalism, confidence | Rehearsals with timed Q&A |

## 7. Presentation and Q&A

- **Timing:** 15 minutes total, including setup and questions. Aim for 10–11 minutes of talk, which leaves 3–4 minutes for Q&A.
- **Split:** each member owns whole sections, for example Overview–Target, Research–Strategy, and Activities–Budget–Impact.
- **Handout:** a one-page campaign summary plus the survey results page. Judges may keep flyers and pamphlets.
- **Allowed materials:** hand-carried visuals only. No power or internet is supplied. Confirm with your advisor whether a battery-powered tablet is OK.
- **Q&A bank:** prepare 15 likely client questions with 30-second answers. Examples: "What's the biggest risk?" "Why these channels?" "What if the conversion is half your target?" "How did you get these numbers?"
