## Slide 4 — I. Overview: Campaign Overview
- Thesis: Pan's does not need more stores - it needs more bags sold per store [S1].
- Problem: stores grew from ~750 to 2,500+ while revenue fell from $3.15M (FY22); that is at most ~6 bags per store per week [S1][S2][S4][A14].
- Opportunity: meat snacks +11.3% to $6.4B while plant-based meat fell 10% - Pan's sits in the wrong mental aisle [S6][S7].
- Solution: 'Switch to Mushroom Mode' puts Pan's in the jerky shopper's decision at 6 H-E-B test stores [O1].
- 45-day goal: +25% units per store vs. control and 4,500 attributed purchases [O1].

*Visual (table):* Four boxes: Problem / Business opportunity / Solution / 45-day goal

> Talk track: Open with the paradox: Pan's went from about 750 stores to 2,500, yet revenue fell. The problem is not distribution - it is what happens at the shelf.

## Slide 5 — II. Product & Company: The Product
- Shiitake mushroom jerky from a decades-old Pan family recipe found in Malaysia; chewy, savory, umami [S2].
- Vegan, gluten-free, Kosher, no allergens to declare; the brand says it 'isn't trying to completely imitate beef' [S5].
- Shelf price $7.99-$8.99 per 2.2 oz bag; cost to make about $1.67 per bag at launch [S3].
- Four core flavors; sold online, on Amazon, and in grocery including H-E-B in Texas [S2][S10].

*Visual (image):* Flat-lay of four flavor bags with a torn-open bag showing the jerky texture

> Talk track: Stress the brand's own words: it is not trying to be beef. That line becomes our whole positioning.

## Slide 6 — II. Product & Company: The Company & Its Position
- Founded by engineer Michael Pan; launched 2018; Mark Cuban invested $300K for 18% on Shark Tank (2020) [S3][S4].
- Revenue $3.15M (FY22) -> $2.88M (FY23) -> $2.78M (11 months FY24); net loss every year [S1].
- Competitors (jerky/meat snacks): Jack Link's $898M (-5.8%), Chomps $797M (+61.5%), Old Trapper (+4%) [S8].
- Position: the only shelf-stable mushroom jerky with national distribution - savory like jerky, honest that it is not beef [S2][S5].

*Visual (chart):* Left: revenue FY22-FY24 falling vs. store count rising. Right: positioning map (x = meat-based to plant-based, y = imitates meat to honest snack) with Jack Link's, Chomps, Old Trapper, plant-based meat brands, and Pan's alone in the 'plant-based + honest snack' quadrant

> Talk track: This is our most important slide for credibility: every number comes from the company's own SEC filing.

## Slide 7 — III. Campaign Objectives: Campaign Objectives

## Slide 8 — IV. Target Market: Target Market
- P1 Young Snack Explorers, 18-29: Central Texas college students and young workers who buy snacks weekly and try new ones from TikTok and friends; skeptical of fake meat [S8][S11].
- P2 Flexitarian H-E-B Parents, 30-50: weekly H-E-B shoppers using app coupons; want snacks with no allergens to worry about [S5][S9].
- Both share one need: a savory snack that is honest about what it is - the reason Pan's says it is not imitating beef [S5].
- Market: 6 in 10 US households already buy plant-based foods [S11].

## Slide 9 — V. Research, Insights & Connection: Research Method & Results
- Secondary (complete): SEC filing, GFI/SPINS, Circana via trade press, company pages - 11 sources [S1][S6][S7][S8].
- Key results: revenue down while stores up [S1][S2]; meat snacks +11.3% [S7]; plant-based meat -10% [S6]; jerky flat while meat sticks surge [S8].
- Primary (in field): 10-question survey of Central Texas snack buyers, 3-5 shopper interviews with taste test, and an H-E-B shelf audit at 3 stores.
- Influences: protein snacking boom (social), plant-based fatigue (cultural), food inflation (economic), app coupons (technological) [S6][S7][S9].

*Visual (chart):* Methods table (4 methods, n, dates, status) + bar chart of category growth: meat snacks +11.3%, plant-based meat -10%

> Talk track: Name the methods and sample sizes out loud. When the survey closes, the bar chart gets our own results.

## Slide 10 — V. Research, Insights & Connection: Insights -> Campaign Decisions
- Finding: store count up, revenue down [S1][S2] -> therefore we fund trial at the shelf (Demo Days), not more distribution.
- Finding: meat snacks +11.3%, plant-based meat -10% [S6][S7] -> therefore Pan's is placed and messaged next to jerky, not with fake meat (Aisle Switch).
- Finding: brand says it is not imitating beef [S5] -> therefore creators run honest taste-offs instead of 'tastes just like beef' claims.
- Finding: losses every year [S1] -> therefore every dollar is trackable (coupons, QR, test vs. control).

*Visual (table):* Four finding -> therefore rows with source tags

> Talk track: Every decision traces to evidence. Point at the arrows, not the text.

## Slide 11 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Campaign Strategy
- Big idea: Mushroom Mode - not fake meat, a savory snack you switch into. Message: 'Love jerky? Switch to Mushroom Mode.'
- Positioning: for Texas snackers who want something savory and honest, Pan's is the jerky made from shiitake that does not pretend to be beef [S5].
- IMC mix: Paid (geo social) + Earned (Shark Tank founder story) + Shared (#MushroomMode duets) + Owned (email/SMS, shelf talkers).
- Push-pull: H-E-B gets a measurable velocity test (push); shoppers get samples and a coupon (pull) [O1][O2].

*Visual (image):* Key visual: split frame - beef jerky aisle on left, Pan's bag glowing on right, 'Switch to Mushroom Mode' lockup

> Talk track: Point to the positioning statement. A competitor that imitates meat cannot say this line - that is what makes it ownable.

## Slide 12 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Program 1: Mushroom Mode Demo Days
- Mushroom Mode in person: 12 weekend demo shifts at 6 Central Texas H-E-B test stores [O1][O2].
- Mechanic: sample cups at the jerky set; QR code to '$2 off 2 bags'; 200 samples per shift = 2,400 total [A7][A8].
- Why: at ~6 bags per store per week, shoppers are walking past - sampling turns curiosity into trial [A14].
- Measured: QR scans per store and units per store vs. control stores [O1].

*Visual (mockup):* Demo table mockup: orange tablecloth, 'Switch to Mushroom Mode' banner, sample cups, QR sign

## Slide 17 — VII. Proposed Budget: Proposed Budget & Financial Viability
- Total $12,000: paid social $5,000 [A4]; creators 4 x $750 = $3,000 [A6]; demos 12 x $200 = $2,400 [A7]; production $800 [A9]; tools $300 [A10]; contingency $500 [A11].
- Funnel: 1.5M impressions x 2% x 15% = 4,500 purchases x $6.00 net (2 bags wholesale minus $2 coupon) = $27K [A1][A4].
- In-window: gross profit $11.9K at 44% = break-even (ROI -1%); ROAS 3.4x [A2].
- 90-day: if 30% of buyers repurchase 2 bags, gross profit reaches ~$18.2K = +51% ROI [A13]. Conservative (50%) 90-day: -24%.
- Upside not counted: ~480 demo-day purchases [A15]. Worst case: a $2.9K velocity test that protects shelf space.

*Visual (table):* Itemized budget table + funnel math + base vs. conservative case

> Talk track: Lead with honesty: in-window it breaks even; the return comes from repeat buyers and from keeping shelf space. Then show the test-vs-control design that proves it.
