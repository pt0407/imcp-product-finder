## Slide 4 — I. Overview: Campaign Overview
- Thesis: Stryve does not need more doors - it needs shoppers who understand what biltong is and come back for it; It's Not Jerky turns H-E-B shelf space into repeat buyers [O1].
- Problem: net sales fell 41% (10-K) while ~1,400 public reviews show only 4% discovered Braav in-store - distribution without velocity [S1][R3].
- Opportunity: meat snacks +11.3% to $6.4B; biltong is the air-dried upgrade sitting in the same set [S6][R2].
- Solution: DFW H-E-B demo days + shelf upgrade + taste-off creators, measured test-vs-control [O1][O2].
- 45-day goal: 2,000 attributed purchases and a velocity readout Stryve can take to buyers [O1].

> Talk track: Open with the paradox: the brand is on more shelves than ever while sales fell 41%. The problem is not distribution - it is what happens at the shelf.

## Slide 5 — II. Product & Company: The Product
- Braav biltong: South African air-dried beef - cured and air-dried, never cooked like jerky; thick, tender, intensely beefy [S10].
- Clean label: sugar-free, gluten-free, keto/paleo/whole30-friendly, 30g+ protein per serving; Original, Steak Bites, and Zero Sugar Hickory lines [S10][S4].
- Retail ~$8-9 per 2.25oz bag (Sprouts $7.99, Walmart multipacks); Plano, TX HQ [S4][S1].
- Sold DTC, on Amazon, and in H-E-B, Walmart, Whole Foods, Sprouts, CVS, Circle K and Love's [S2][S3].

*Visual (image):* Real Stryve Original pouch + bites product photography

> Talk track: The key sensory difference: jerky is cooked and shredded; biltong is air-dried whole muscle - denser, richer, chewier in a different way.

## Slide 6 — II. Product & Company: The Company & Its Position
- Stryve Foods Corp (NASDAQ: SNAX), Plano TX; founded by Ted Casey (Dymatize), ex-NFL player Gabe Carimi, and South African native Warren Pala [S1][S10].
- Net sales $29.9M -> $17.7M (-41%) in FY2023; 10-K cites discontinuing 'non-profitable accounts' and rationalizing 'low-quality revenue' [S1].
- Competitors in Texas biltong are fragmented regional players (Brooklyn Biltong, Shaka's, Billi Tong's, Lowfeld Soul Food); Stryve is the only national brand with H-E-B scale [S5].
- Position: premium air-dried beef for performance and clean-label shoppers - the jerky upgrade, not another jerky [S10][R2].

## Slide 8 — IV. Target Market: Target Market
- P1 Performance Snackers, 18-34: DFW/Texas jerky buyers focused on macros; keto/paleo/whole30-curious; buy on protein and clean labels [S6][R2].
- P2 Clean-Label H-E-B Parents, 30-50: weekly H-E-B shoppers wanting no-sugar, recognizable-ingredient snacks for lunchboxes [S6][S9].
- Both share one need: a meat snack that is clearly better than jerky - the reason 12% of reviewers cite clean-label benefits and 5% compare it directly [R2].
- Market: meat snacks +11.3% to $6.4B; high-protein snack positioning is the fastest-growing claim set in the aisle [S6][S8].

## Slide 9 — V. Research, Insights & Connection: Research Method & Results
- Secondary (complete): 10-K via last10k, company site/stores, Walmart/Sprouts listings, trade press - 10 sources [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5].
- Primary, no-survey (complete): keyword-coded analysis of 1,400 public Stamped.io reviews [R1][R4] and a public distribution/digital-shelf audit of 8 sources [R5][R6] [S11][S12]; survey/interviews not run - published reviews skew positive.
- Key results: sales -41% while doors multiplied [S1][R5]; taste and reorder dominate reviews [R1][R4]; only 4% discovered it in a store [R3]; biltong is an unfamiliar word in Texas [R6].
- Influences: protein-snack boom (social), clean-label/keto trend (cultural), meat-snack growth +11.3% (market), retail app coupons (technological) [S6][S7][S9].

## Slide 10 — V. Research, Insights & Connection: Insights -> Campaign Decisions
- Finding: 1,400-review analysis - taste/flavor 29%, repeat/bulk 20%, only 4% found in store [R1][R3] -> in-store education, not more awareness ads.
- Finding: SEC 10-K documents -41% sales and 'low-quality revenue' rationalization [S1] -> the campaign must produce sell-through proof, not impressions.
- Finding: meat-snack category +11.3% while Stryve declines [S6] -> ride the tailwind with the air-dried upgrade positioning [R2].
- Finding: reviews skew 5-star but 'found in store' is near-zero [R1][R3] -> demos + shelf talkers at the point of trial.
- Finding: Texas HQ + DFW H-E-B doors [S2][S9] -> the test market is walkable and measurable.

## Slide 11 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Campaign Strategy
- Big idea: It's Not Jerky - biltong is air-dried, never cooked; a different product for a different shopper. Message: 'Love jerky? Try biltong.'
- Positioning: for performance and clean-label shoppers who want more from a meat snack, Braav is the air-dried beef upgrade [S10][R2].
- IMC mix, all testable: Paid geo-social -> store traffic; Earned Plano founder/ex-NFL story -> H-E-B buyer pitch; Shared #ItsNotJerky taste-off duets -> proof; Owned email/SMS + shelf talkers -> repeat [O1][O2].
- Push-pull: H-E-B gets a measurable velocity test (push); shoppers get samples and a coupon (pull) [O1][O2].

*Visual (image):* Key visual: split frame - cooked jerky strips on left, thick air-dried biltong slab on right, 'It's Not Jerky' lockup

> Talk track: Point to the positioning statement. 'Air-dried, not cooked' is a factual difference no jerky brand can claim.

## Slide 12 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Program 1: Biltong 101 Demo Days
- It's Not Jerky in person: 12 weekend Biltong 101 demo shifts at 6 DFW H-E-B test stores; 6 control stores get the coupon only [O1][O2].
- Mechanic: 'What is biltong?' sample cups at the jerky/meat-snack set; QR to '$2 off 2 bags'; 300 samples per shift = 3,600 total [A7][A8].
- Why: biltong is an unfamiliar word in Texas [R6] and only 4% of reviewers found it in a store [R3] - the shelf is where education must happen.
- Measured: QR scans per store and units per store vs. control stores [O1].

*Visual (mockup):* Demo table mockup: dark red tablecloth, 'It's Not Jerky - It's Biltong' banner, sample cups, QR sign

## Slide 17 — VII. Proposed Budget: Proposed Budget & Financial Viability
- Total $15,000: social $5,500 [A4]; creators 3 x $1,000 [A6]; demos 12 x $250 [A7]; production $900 [A9]; tools $400 [A10]; contingency $2,200 [A11].
- Funnel: 1.5M x 1.5% x 10% = 2,250 purchases at $18 avg = $40.5K [A1][A4].
- The $2 coupon is manufacturer-funded, netted in revenue [A1][A8].
- GP 45% = $18.2K; ROI 21%; ROAS 4.8x; CAC $6.67 vs CLV ~$23 = 1:3.5 [A2][A13].
- Conservative (50%): ROI ~-40%; ~720 demo purchases +$5.8K GP [A15] + repeat tail [A13] -> breakeven ~wk 10-12; plan-B doors if demos declined [A5][A18].
- Margin sensitivity: at 35% GP (10-K low end) ROI ~-6%; breakeven margin ~37% - upside case holds at 45% [A2][S1].

*Visual (image):* 

> Talk track: Lead with honesty: conservative case loses in-window; the return comes from repeat buyers and from keeping shelf space the 10-K is rationalizing. Then show the test-vs-control design.
