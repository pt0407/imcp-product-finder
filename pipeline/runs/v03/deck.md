## Slide 4 — I. Overview: Campaign Overview
- Thesis: "Borogodó, Delivered: turn tinyB's 82,000 annual workshop attendees into customers." [S5]
- Problem: ~57% of tinyB revenue comes from corporate events - a capacity-limited channel - while consumer DTC gifting is only ~14% [S5].
- Business opportunity: kits reach 82,000+ participants yearly with no purchase path; Valentine's is a record $29.1B occasion where candy is the top gift [S2][S5].
- Solution: 'Borogodó, Delivered' puts a consumer offer inside every corporate kit, samples at the flagship store, and launches a limited Valentine's Brigadeiro Box [O1].
- 45-day goal: 1,000 new DTC orders and 5,000 new email/SMS subscribers [O1][O2].

*Visual (table):* Four boxes: Problem / Business opportunity / Solution / 45-day goal

> Talk track: The key line: tinyB's best marketing already happens - inside corporate workshops - but it ends with a handshake, not a sale. We fix the last inch.

## Slide 5 — II. Product & Company: The Product
- Handmade Brazilian brigadeiros - gluten-free bite-sized chocolates made in small batches in San Mateo [S5][S6].
- Sold as singles, gift boxes, and experience kits; company average order ~$47 [S5].
- Margins of 72-78% across channels - gifting and DTC carry the highest margins [S5].
- 4.8 Google / 5.0 Yelp ratings across 30,000+ customers served [S5].

*Visual (image):* Assorted brigadeiro flavors in brand packaging on marble

> Talk track: Brigadeiro is unknown in the US - that is the problem and the opportunity. A $29B chocolate category [S1] has never heard the word.

## Slide 6 — II. Product & Company: The Company & Its Position
- Founded 2014 by Renata Stoica, a Brazilian former nurse; ~40 employees; profitable and bootstrapped, raising $1.5M [S5][S7].
- Revenue ~$916K in 2025, +30% YoY; mix: 57% corporate events, 22% store, 14% DTC gifting, 7% wholesale [S5].
- Clients include Google, Cisco, Meta, Salesforce, PwC, Intuit - 3,200+ events in 52+ countries [S5].
- Position: premium experiential chocolate - priced above mass-market, below luxury gifting [S5][S8].

*Visual (chart):* Revenue mix donut (57/22/14/7) + timeline 2014-2027

> Talk track: This is a healthy, growing company - which is exactly why the pitch works: they do not need a rescue, they need a bridge from B2B love to consumer revenue.

## Slide 8 — IV. Target Market: Target Market
- P1 'The Gifter': 25-45 professionals who already ate brigadeiros at a tinyB corporate event; buys gifts 2-4x/year; reachable by email and office word-of-mouth [S5][S8].
- P2 'The Sweet-Seeker': 18-28 Gen-Z dessert loyalists; discovers food brands on TikTok; values gluten-free and founder stories [S4][S6].
- Shared truth: edible gifts are the most-appreciated corporate gift (81%) and recipients are 79% more likely to remember the giver [S8].

*Visual (mockup):* Two persona cards with photos, demographics, psychographics, media habits

> Talk track: P1 is the unfair advantage - we are not buying this audience, the brand already taught them what a borogodó is. P2 is the growth audience.

## Slide 9 — V. Research, Insights & Connection: Research Method & Results
- Secondary research (complete): company investor brief, GlobalData, NRF, NCA, Circana/State of Treating, Giftsenda/Coresight [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5][S8].
- Key results: US chocolate market $29.2B and growing 5.7%/yr [S1]; Valentine's hits a record $29.1B with candy the top gift [S2][S3]; 99.8% of households buy confectionery, avg $352/yr [S4].
- Primary research (in field): 8-question survey of chocolate/gift buyers + 3-5 interviews with past corporate-event attendees; results load into slides 10 and 17 before submission [R-pending].
- Environmental scan: cocoa prices and tariffs are squeezing chocolate COGS - making tinyB's asset-light gifting model (no grocery slotting) strategically attractive [S4].

*Visual (chart):* Methods table + key stats bar chart (Valentine's $29.1B, chocolate $29.2B, household penetration 99.8%)

> Talk track: The chocolate category is huge but mature at 2.6% growth [S1] - tinyB's edge is not the category, it is the unclaimed brigadeiro niche and the owned audience.

## Slide 11 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Campaign Strategy
- Big idea 'Borogodó, Delivered': the little extra that makes something special - tinyB's own word - now delivered from work event to home gift box.
- IMC mix: Paid (TikTok/Meta) + Earned (founder story, PR) + Shared (UGC challenge) + Owned (kit inserts, email/SMS, POS).
- Push-pull: corporate clients get a seamless gifting story (push); consumers get sampling, creator proof, and a limited box (pull); one QR code connects both.
- Ownable element: only tinyB can put borogodó inside the kits it already runs - a competitor would have to buy an audience this brand already owns [S5].

*Visual (image):* Key visual: brigadeiro box with 'Borogodó, Delivered' lockup and the word's translation card

> Talk track: Theme check: 'Borogodó' appears on every activity slide. It is tinyB's own vocabulary - the campaign sounds like the brand, not like an agency.

## Slide 13 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Program 1: The Corporate Bridge
- Borogodó, Delivered starts inside the kits tinyB already ships: a 'Take Borogodó Home' card with QR code + 20% off first DTC order [O1][O2].
- Mechanic: card goes into every corporate event kit from week 1; scan lands on a kit-specific page that captures email/SMS consent.
- Why it works: 82,000+ participants already experienced the product - the warmest possible audience, at near-zero media cost [S5].
- Measurement: per-kit QR codes attribute every order; target 800 scans and 150 redemptions [A5].

*Visual (mockup):* Insert card mockup: 'You just made borogodó at work - take it home. Scan for 20% off' + QR

> Talk track: This is the strategic centerpiece. Frame it as proposed: the client approves adding the card to kits - we are not claiming it already exists.

## Slide 14 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Program 2: Brigadeiro Bar
- Borogodó, Delivered goes sensory: weekend tasting bars at the San Mateo flagship and 3 proposed Bay Area cafe partners [O1][O2].
- Mechanic: guests taste 3 brigadeiros, get a stamped loyalty card, and leave with the gifting-box offer and a QR code.
- Target: 600 tastings over 5 weeks at ~$8 product cost each [A7].
- Why it works: brigadeiro is unknown in the US [S6] - tasting converts curiosity faster than any ad.

*Visual (mockup):* Pop-up bar mockup: tasting cups, branded napkins, 'Borogodó, Delivered' banner

> Talk track: Sampling is the highest-trust channel for a novel food. The stamped card turns a one-time taste into a follow-up.

## Slide 15 — VI. Strategy, Activities & Schedule: Program 3: #ShareTheBorogodo + Valentine's Box
- Borogodó, Delivered gets its moment: limited Valentine's Brigadeiro Box launches week 3 for Galentine's and Valentine's Day [O1].
- UGC challenge: post your gifting or tasting moment with #ShareTheBorogodo; 5 weekly winners get a box; entries capture email/SMS [O2][O3].
- Named decision-maker: founder Renata Stoica fronts 'Renata's Kitchen' - the brigadeiro origin story and what borogodó means [S5][S6].
- 6 food/lifestyle micro-creators seed the unboxing format weeks 1-3 so the challenge has examples on day one [O3].

*Visual (mockup):* Gift box mockup + TikTok phone frame showing unboxing with #ShareTheBorogodo caption

> Talk track: Valentine's timing is deliberate: record $29.1B spending [S2] and candy is the most-planned gift [S3] - the box gives the campaign a reason to exist now.

## Slide 17 — VII. Proposed Budget: Proposed Budget & Financial Viability
- Total $19,000: media $6,500 [A4]; creators 6 x $700 = $4,200 [A6]; Brigadeiro Bar $3,000 + sampling $2,000 [A7]; insert cards $1,000 [A5]; email/SMS $800 [A9]; PR $300 [A10]; landing/QR $200 [A11]; contingency $1,000 [A12].
- Funnel: 2.5M impressions x 1.2% CTR = 30K clicks x 4.5% conv = 1,350 orders x $47 AOV = $63K revenue [A1][A4].
- GP at 72% margin = $45.7K; profit ROI 140%; ROAS 5.9x on $10.7K ad spend [A2].
- Break-even 561 orders; base 1,350, conservative (50%) 675 - clears break-even [O1].
- CAC $14.07 base / $28.15 conservative vs. $47 AOV at 72% margin [A1][A2].

*Visual (table):* Itemized budget table (9 lines with cost basis) + funnel math + base/conservative case

> Talk track: The insert card costs ~$1K and reaches 82,000 warm prospects - the cheapest reach in the plan. Lead with that ratio.
