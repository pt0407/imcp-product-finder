# IMCP Delivery & Q&A Prep (district → ICDC)

Sourced from r/DECA ICDC-2026 attendee threads + DECA Direct judge articles. Deck intel in KNOWLEDGE.md §15.

## Format facts (verified by competitors)
- Judges read the deck BEFORE you present — every slide must stand alone; don't rely on you saying the missing part.
- 15:00 hard cutoff with a loud time warning; ~1 minute for Q&A at ICDC. Rehearse to 10–11 minutes, never 14.
- Room: warehouse hall, judge(s) on one side of a table, team opposite with laptop facing them. Sometimes 2–3 judges.
- Bring a physical prop: product prototype/packaging + a printed shelf talker + a QR table card. Judges picked up a prototype and scanned a QR code 3x at ICDC. Skip poster boards.
- EIP note: this year's Innovation Plan is also a pitch deck; a working prototype matters even more there.

## The 15 hardest judge questions (answer framework: acknowledge → number → tie to objective)

1. **"Whose idea was this campaign — yours or the client's?"**
   → "Our agency team developed it from their research; we validated every decision against their 10-K decline and 1,400-review corpus. The client owns the product; we own the strategy." (Never claim the client asked for it — it's a proposal.)

2. **"What happens if H-E-B says no to the clip strip?"**
   → Deck slide 13: demos + printable coupon still run the test — the leg shrinks, not collapses. Control stores still measure lift.

3. **"Your conservative case loses ~40% ROI. Why should we fund it?"**
   → In-window it breaks even at best; the return is repeat purchase (20% of reviewers already reorder) + proving velocity to keep shelf space the 10-K is cutting. Show the test-vs-control design proves causation.

4. **"How do you know the $2 coupon is funded?"**
   → Assumption [A8]: manufacturer-funded, netted in blended revenue. Ask the client on day one; if unfunded, swap to a smaller offer.

5. **"Why these 6 stores? How are control stores matched?"**
   → Matched on banner, size tier, and baseline velocity measured week 0 before any spend [A3].

6. **"Your review corpus is all 5-star — isn't that biased?"**
   → Yes — self-selected buyers skew positive; we state the limitation on slide 9 and use it only for themes (taste/repeat), never for market sizing.

7. **"What does 'biltong' mean to someone who's never heard it?"**
   → South African air-dried beef — denser and richer than jerky, never cooked. That's why the campaign leads with demos and taste-offs, not ads [R6].

8. **"Where do you see this brand in 3–5 years?"** (seen verbatim at ICDC)
   → "From a $17.7M brand recovering from a 41% decline to the premium air-dried snack set — proven by the same test-vs-control velocity data this campaign generates."

9. **"What's your margin? Show me the unit economics."**
   → Slide 17: $18 blended net revenue, 45% gross margin, $6.67 CAC, 4.76x ROAS on ad spend. Conservative case shown honestly.

10. **"Why not just spend the $15K on Amazon ads?"**
    → Amazon ratings already lag (3.0–4.2 vs 4.95 site) — paid traffic to a weak rating page burns money. Fix trial-to-conviction at the shelf first [R5][R6].

11. **"How is this 'integrated'? Anyone can run TikTok ads."**
    → Paid + earned (founder story) + shared (creator duets) + owned (email/SMS/shelf) all point to one KPI: units per store vs. control. Every channel has a job in the funnel.

12. **"What if creators don't show the product honestly?"**
    → #ad disclosure required; we brief them on the real story (air-dried, not cooked) — the campaign's credibility depends on honest taste-offs.

13. **"How do you attribute the H-E-B coupon sales to the campaign?"**
    → Unique coupon code + QR scans per store + matched control stores — three independent measures of the same lift [O1].

14. **"This is a 45-day campaign — what lasts after it ends?"**
    → The velocity data package Stryve takes to every retailer + the review corpus the demos generate + repeat-buyers from the email/SMS list [A13].

15. **"What did you NOT do that you wanted to?"** (integrity question)
    → We didn't run a consumer survey or interviews — reviews skew positive, so we capped research honestly at the lite tier instead of faking primary data.

## Delivery checklist
- [ ] 10–11 min runtime; watch on the lectern; practice the cutoff at 15:00
- [ ] Hand prop on table before you start: product bag, shelf talker printout, QR card (make sure it scans!)
- [ ] One speaker per section — split evenly; everyone must present
- [ ] Memorize the thesis line verbatim; open AND close with it
- [ ] If you blank: pause, breathe, jump to the next bullet — never apologize mid-flow
- [ ] Close by asking the judge to adopt the plan ("closing the sale")
