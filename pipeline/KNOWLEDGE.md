# IMCP Marketing Knowledge Base

Read this file before every generate, grade, or revise task. It contains the marketing knowledge a strong IMCP deck depends on, plus the patterns observed in the 2026 ICDC 1st/2nd/3rd-place decks.

---

## 0. The frame: this is an agency pitch

- You are **account managers at a marketing agency**. The judge is a **prospective client** deciding whether to hire you. (Official 2026–27 guidelines.)
- Write in client language: "We recommend…", "Your customers…", "This gets you…". Not "Our project is about…".
- A client cares about three things: **Will it solve my problem? Will it pay back? Can I trust these people?** Every slide should answer one of them.

---

## 1. Business problem framing (Overview and Business Opportunity)

**Rule:** state a **specific structural problem**, not "low awareness."

| Weak (scores low) | Strong (scores high) |
|---|---|
| "Brand lacks awareness." | "95% of revenue comes from corporate orders; the consumer channel is under 5% and shrinking." |
| "We want more sales." | "Direct sales are a small share of revenue; most revenue comes from rentals." (2nd place) |
| "Expand online presence." | "Sold only B2B despite consumer demand." (1st place) |

Types of structural problems: channel gap (B2B-only, retail-only, DTC-only), revenue concentration, new market or segment entry, new product launch, seasonal dependence, low repeat rate, price-perception gap, lapsed customers.

The 3rd-place deck used "lack of brand positioning/awareness." Its overview was its weakest criterion at prelims (30th percentile).

**Overview slide formula (used by all three winners):** four boxes: Problem · Business need/opportunity · Solution (campaign name) · Measurable 45-day goal.

---

## 2. Objectives

- **SMART:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound (within the ≤45-day window).
- Map each objective to a **funnel stage**:
  - Awareness: reach, impressions, aided awareness
  - Consideration: site visits, email sign-ups, engagement rate
  - Conversion: orders, trials, sign-ups
  - Loyalty: repeat purchase, subscriptions, referrals
- Use **one primary objective** (usually conversion or trial) and 2–3 supporting objectives.
- Give each objective an ID (O1, O2, O3) and reference it on activity, budget, and KPI slides. Criterion: "objectives referenced throughout."
- The 1st place spelled its objectives as an acrostic of the theme (S-A-V-E). Memorable, but optional.

---

## 3. Target market

**Segmentation bases (use all four):**
- **Demographic:** age, income, education, life stage
- **Geographic:** region, urban/suburban, climate, specific metros
- **Psychographic:** values, interests, lifestyle, attitudes
- **Behavioral:** purchase occasion, usage rate, benefits sought, loyalty, channel habits

**Rules:**
- Primary and secondary segments. A tertiary segment is fine if it has a role, like the 2nd place's NGOs and partners.
- **Personas must come from data.** Use a name, age, job, pain points, and media habits, and cite the survey result that supports each. The 1st place used two personas, Alice (34) and Jason (25).
- Size the market when possible: **TAM** (total addressable) → **SAM** (serviceable) → **SOM** (what this campaign can realistically reach). Cite sources.
- **Media consumption patterns are required by the 2026–27 research section.** State where each segment spends time, and source it.

## 4. Research (15 points, new this year, and the winners skipped it)

### Primary vs. secondary
- **Primary:** data you collect: surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, social listening, taste or product tests, A/B tests.
- **Secondary:** published data: industry reports, government data, trade press, company filings, news.
- Judges score the **method**, the **results**, and **how findings inform decisions**. Secondary data alone caps this category at 5/15.

### Survey design rules
- 6–10 questions, under 3 minutes.
- Start with a **screener** ("Do you buy [category] at least once a month?").
- One concept per question. No leading questions ("How much do you love…?" is bad).
- Use scales (1–5 Likert) for attitudes and multiple choice for behavior and channels.
- Include: awareness of the brand, purchase drivers, where they discover products (channels), price sensitivity (Van Westendorp-style: "too cheap / good value / expensive / too expensive"), and an open-ended "why."
- Report **n**, collection dates, and recruitment method. Be honest about limits ("convenience sample, n=64, skews 16–24").

### Interview rules
- 3–5 interviews, 10–15 minutes, open questions ("Tell me about the last time you bought…").
- Pull 2–3 direct quotes for slides. Quotes are strong creative material.

### Environmental scan (required "influences")
The guidelines name **cultural, social, economic, technological** influences and **media consumption patterns**. Use a short PESTLE-style table (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental), keeping only rows that matter.

### Competitor audit
3–5 competitors × price, channel, positioning, and message. Show a **positioning map** (two axes, such as price vs. indulgence) with the white space marked.

### No-survey primary methods (fallback when the team can't collect a survey)
Judges still reward real data collection. If a survey/interviews are impossible, complete **at least two** of these and label them honestly:
- **Review analysis** (`review_analysis`, n≥100): keyword-coded content analysis of public customer reviews. Customer-voice data, not a survey — state that reviewers self-select and skew positive.
- **Store observation** (`store_observation`, n≥3): in-person visits recording shelf placement, facings, price tags, competitor adjacency.
- **Digital shelf audit** (`digital_shelf_audit`, n≥3): the same audit on retailer sites (placement, review counts, price vs. competitors).
- **Social listening** (`social_listening`, n≥100): coded public posts/comments about the brand or category.
Two completed lite methods cap Research at 10/15. A real survey or 3 interviews unlock 11–15. Never blur the distinction on the slide.

### The format that earns points
> **Finding** (method, n, source) → **Therefore** we will [specific campaign decision]

Every major decision (segment, channel, message, offer, timing) must trace back to a finding. Tag research findings [R1], [R2], and so on, and cite them on the strategy slides.

## 5. Positioning and value proposition

**Positioning statement template:**
> For [target segment] who [need or pain], [brand] is the [frame of reference / category] that [key point of difference] because [reason to believe].

- **Points of parity:** what you must match to be considered at all (taste, price range).
- **Points of difference:** why you win. Must be **relevant, distinctive, and believable**.
- **Reason to believe:** proof (certification, founder story, data, testimonials).
- **Value proposition:** benefit to the customer relative to the price.

## 6. Strategy: integrated marketing communications (IMC)

**IMC** means one consistent message across all channels, each channel doing a specific job. "Integrated" is in the event name, and judges look for it.

### One big idea
- A single campaign concept (name + theme) that solves the structural problem.
- All three winners had one: "Save What Matters," "Racing Toward a Greener Future / Race for our Oceans," and "#BeAMisfit."
- **Test:** could a competitor run this exact campaign? If yes, it isn't original enough. Add something only this brand can own: its founder story, an unusual product feature, a partner, or a mission metric.

### Promotional mix (name the elements you use)
Advertising · Public relations · Sales promotion · Personal selling · Direct marketing · Digital/social marketing.

### PESO model (for channel planning)
- **Paid:** ads, sponsored creator posts, retail media
- **Earned:** press, reviews, word of mouth
- **Shared:** social posts, UGC, communities
- **Owned:** website, email list, SMS list, packaging, stores

A strong plan uses all four, with owned channels capturing the audience that paid and earned channels attract.

### Push vs. pull
- **Push:** motivate the channel (retailers, distributors) to stock and promote: trade promotions, demos, co-op ads.
- **Pull:** motivate consumers to ask for or seek out the product: ads, social, sampling.
- B2B or retail-channel products usually need both.

### Message frameworks
- **AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Use it to order content within the campaign.
- **Customer journey:** awareness → consideration → purchase → retention → advocacy. Assign each activity to a stage.

## 7. Activities and schedule

**Phases inside 45 days:**
1. **Tease** (days 1–7): build curiosity, seed creators, capture emails. The 2nd place used "Something green is coming."
2. **Launch** (days 8–14): hero event or drop, PR, paid push.
3. **Sustain** (days 15–35): UGC challenge, sampling, partner activations, retargeting.
4. **Convert** (days 36–42): limited-time offer, urgency, final email and SMS push.
5. **Close** (days 43–45): results recap, thank-you, retention handoff.

**Every activity needs:** name · segment · channel · objective (O#) · week · owner. Present it as a Gantt chart (weeks × channels).

**Named programs beat generic tactics.**
- Strong: "Earth Day Trial Fleet: 10 clubs × 4 buoys at 15% off, SMS check-ins on days 1 and 3, target 80% trial-to-purchase" (2nd place).
- Weak: "Social media campaign."

## 8. Creative samples

- **Show, don't describe.** Phone mockups with real copy (SMS threads, Instagram posts, TikTok frames), packaging, POS displays, email screenshots, event visuals.
- Every creative piece shows the **theme**, a **clear CTA**, and the **brand**.
- **Copy rules:** one message per piece, benefit before feature, specific numbers, a CTA verb ("Scan," "Join," "Claim").
- **Label AI-generated images** as such. The 2nd place did this on the slide.

### Compliance (clients notice when you get this right)
- **Influencers:** FTC requires clear disclosure (#ad or "Paid partnership").
- **Contests and sweepstakes:** "No purchase necessary," official rules, eligibility, and end date. A purchase requirement can turn a sweepstakes into an illegal lottery.
- **SMS:** requires prior express written consent (TCPA) and opt-out instructions ("Reply STOP").
- **Email:** CAN-SPAM requires an unsubscribe link and a physical address.
- **Under-13 audiences:** COPPA limits data collection. Avoid targeting children online.
- **Health claims** on food and supplements must be supportable.

## 9. Budget and financial viability

### Line items
Group lines into: media (paid social, search, retail media), creators/talent, production (content, design), events/sampling (venue, staff, product cost/COGS), promotions (discount cost, prizes), tools (email/SMS software), PR, and contingency (5–10%).

Every line needs a **cost basis**: a vendor quote, a sourced benchmark, or a labeled assumption. The 2nd place itemized down to $29 SMS software.

### Funnel math (how revenue projections should be built)
```
Impressions × CTR = Clicks
Clicks × Conversion rate = Orders
Orders × AOV = Revenue
Revenue × Gross margin = Gross profit
```
Build the projection bottom-up from these numbers, not top-down from a target.

### Key formulas (use the right one)
- **ROI** = (Gross profit attributable to the campaign − Campaign cost) ÷ Campaign cost
- **ROAS** = Revenue ÷ Ad spend (a revenue ratio, not ROI)
- **CAC** = Campaign acquisition spend ÷ New customers
- **Break-even orders** = Campaign cost ÷ (AOV × Gross margin)
- **CPM** = Cost per 1,000 impressions · **CPC** = Cost per click · **CTR** = Clicks ÷ Impressions
- **LTV** (simple) = AOV × Gross margin × Expected purchases per customer

**Winner observation:** the 1st and 2nd places computed "ROI" as (Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost, with no margin. Judges didn't penalize it. Showing **both ROAS and profit-based ROI** is more correct and sets you apart. Also show a **conservative case** (for example, half the conversion rate) and confirm it still breaks even or explain the risk.

## 10. Impact and measurement

**KPI table columns:** Objective (O#) · KPI · Baseline · Target · Tracking tool · Check cadence.

**Tracking tools to name:**
- **UTM parameters** on every link (which channel drove the visit)
- **Unique promo codes** per creator, event, or channel
- **QR codes** on print and POS pieces
- **Dedicated landing page** for the campaign
- **Ad platform analytics** (Meta, TikTok, Google)
- **Pre/post brand-lift survey** (awareness and perception change)
- **Social listening** (sentiment, mentions)
- **Sales dashboard** (Shopify, POS)

**Leading vs. lagging indicators:**
- Leading: engagement, CTR, email sign-ups. Check weekly and optimize.
- Lagging: sales, repeat rate. Report at the end.

**Optimization triggers** (shows you think like an agency): "If CTR is under 0.8% by day 10, shift 20% of the budget from Meta to TikTok."

**Avoid vanity-only metrics.** Followers and likes alone don't prove business impact. Pair each one with a conversion metric.

**Mission metric:** both top decks quantified real-world impact (100,000 lbs of fruit saved; 1.2 tons of CO2 prevented). If the brand has a mission, measure it.

## 11. Presentation (35 points)

- **Structure:** hook (customer quote or startling finding) → problem → research → idea → plan → money → results → close on the mission.
- **Thesis:** one sentence, said at the open and the close.
- **Interactive techniques** (the delivery criterion names them): hand the judge a printed creative sample, walk through a phone mockup, show a survey chart, ask a rhetorical question. No food or drinks are allowed.
- **Timing:** 15 minutes total, including setup and Q&A. Aim for 10–11 minutes of talk.
- **Every team member presents** and answers questions.
- **Q&A method:** acknowledge → answer with a number or finding → bridge back to the objective. Never bluff; say "We'd validate that in week 1 with…"
- **Handout:** a one-page summary plus survey results. Judges may keep flyers and pamphlets.

## 14. Common mistakes (each one costs points)

1. Generic "awareness" problem
2. Secondary data only, with no method stated
3. Insights that don't change any decision
4. Generic "influencers + UGC + ads" strategy with no ownable element
5. Activities without a segment, channel, or objective attached
6. Budget lines with no cost basis; totals that don't sum
7. Revenue-only "ROI"; no conservative case
8. KPIs without targets or baselines
9. Text-heavy slides (over 110 words)
10. Unsourced numbers
11. Penalties: over 20 slides, wrong numbering or order, missing bibliography, unsigned Statement of Assurances

## 15. Judge intel (sourced, not Reddit)

r/DECA is effectively empty — DECA advice lives on DECA Direct, state DECA sites, LinkedIn, and winner write-ups. Verified sources:

- **"In the Judge's Mind" (DECA Direct):** first 10 seconds decide the room — confident greeting, eye contact, match formality to the judge's role. Judges reward "realistic business thinking": who/what/when/how, costs, timelines, measurable benefit. Strongest decks "tell a cohesive story": restate the issue, name the gap, show the solution fills it. Don't insult the client's current strategy — pitch as enhancement, not fix of their failure.
- **"Tips from Past Winners" (California DECA PDF):** end by closing the sale — summarize best points, ask the judge to adopt the plan. Judges sit through dozens of decks; uniqueness + confidence beat polish.
- **Cengage judge article:** never define textbook terms without applying them to THIS scenario; pitching an idea as fixing the company's past failure insults leadership — frame as a new opportunity.
- **Andrew Weatherman (ex-DECA Exec President, Medium):** don't center the campaign on a gimmick — judges take it less seriously. The two months between state and ICDC separate finalists: know the scenario space, adjust plans to variables, and ground every plan in "real-world" feasibility (who executes, what it costs).
- **decaplus.org sample role-plays:** official IMCP scenarios — read before delivery practice.
