Source data: bloomnu.com (Shopify + Okendo/Bazaarvoice reviews), LinkedIn, Forbes, Inc., Nutrabolt press release, CB Insights.

**Total: 28/40** — but fails the spirit of criterion 1+7.

## One-sentence business problem
Bloom's core greens business is being eclipsed by its own beverage explosion — the brand must decide whether it stays "greens for women" or becomes a functional-beverage platform, and shoppers don't yet know it for the new categories.

## Campaign angle to test first
"The Second Bloom" — reposition around the beverage platform (energy + soda + hydration sticks) while defending greens equity; or a trial-conversion play in a single DM_DMA test market vs. Celsius/Prime/Alani.

## Top open questions
1. Is greens revenue actually declining, or just being outgrown? (Needs earnings/Nutrabolt commentary — may not be public.)
2. Which category is the strategic priority for 2027 — energy, soda, or hydration?
3. Will they even take a student agency pitch? (NVIDIA precedent says judges reward famous brands regardless.)

## Verdict: SKIP (borderline MAYBE)
Bloom is a $300M marketing machine — criterion 7 fails hard: there is no whitespace an agency can own, and the "structural problem" is inferred, not evidenced. Under the NVIDIA precedent a famous brand *can* win on recognition alone, but that deck scored under the old rubric with no research section; this year you'd need real research on a brand whose data is locked in Okendo/enterprise platforms. Only revisit if you find a documented sales problem (e.g., a category decline or retail velocity issue) — until then, Stryve v11 (85) and BEEST v13 (82) remain stronger.
